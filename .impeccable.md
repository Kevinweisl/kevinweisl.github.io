# Design Context

## Target Audience
Academic peers, potential collaborators, graduate students, and the ccClub community (4,000+ learners). Visitors are primarily researchers in NLP/IR, faculty, and industry professionals exploring collaboration.

## Use Cases
- Learn about Kevin's research and publications
- Find contact information for collaboration
- Read Kevin's notes/articles on research and community building
- Understand Kevin's background (academia + 6 years industry)

## Brand Personality / Tone
- **Professional but approachable**: Academic rigor without being cold or intimidating
- **Knowledgeable but warm**: Bridges research and real-world applications
- **Distinctive but not flashy**: Memorable without being distracting from content

## Existing Brand Colors

Sampled from the assets themselves, never from memory, with each colour's share
of the mark — the share is what tells you whether a colour is a lead or a
garnish, so record it.

**The site's anchor is Kevin's own mark**, `public/avatar.ico`, the KW monogram
that sits in the hero card:

- Sky blue `#A0CCE7` — 72% of the mark
- Near-black `#171E1D` — 12%

The palette answers it: the accent *is* that sky blue, and the ground `#06090B`
lands at hue 204.0 against the monogram's 202.8, so the two needed no
reconciling. The whole token set sits between 197 and 204 degrees; only the
low-saturation body and muted greys sit at 193.

**ccClub's logo** is the historical anchor and no longer the primary one:

- Ice blue `#CADFE3` — 72.7% of the mark
- Teal `#487981` — 9.1%
- Pink `#E288B3` — 7.0%

That asset is not in this repo and appears on no page, while `#A0CCE7` was on
screen the whole time with no token pointing at it. ccClub survives in the
palette as the hero's primary CTA fill (`--hero-cta-bg: #CADFE3`) and as the
pink, which is a second brand colour rather than a garnish because pink and
teal are near-equal in area.

Never record a brand colour from memory: this file once named the second colour
as Coral Red `#D24E42`, which does not appear in the logo at all, and that error
kept pink out of the site for a full redesign cycle.

### Colour roles (2026-09-06)

One colour, one job. Sky blue `--accent` `#A0CCE7` means **clickable** — links,
buttons, hover and focus states — and nothing else. Non-clickable text never
wears it, which is why the instrument rail is muted.

Pink `--brand` `#EAA9C8` has exactly four jobs: the author's own name in a
byline, the emphasised word in a section title, an in-progress marker
(`Present`), and the hero's italic links — the one place the brand colour
carries a link. Two former uses were removed in this cycle as violations that
had gone unnoticed: an ambient hero blob, and the hero card's label.

Raw `#E288B3` fails AA as text. The text pink keeps the logo's hue and
saturation and moves only lightness to reach 4.5:1.

Labels use the `.label` utility — 11px, 600, uppercase, tracked, `--text-muted`,
**and the mono family**, which it owns rather than borrows. They are told apart
from body text by case, tracking and family, so colour stays free for
interaction. The text flowing through `.label` must stay Latin: JetBrains Mono
has no CJK and would fall back mid-string. `design-system.test.ts` guards it.

Surfaces: one ground, no alternating section backgrounds. Sections separate with
a 1px `--border` hairline. `--border` is `#33474F` — 1.81 against a card, 2.05
against the page. A hovered row wears `--bg-card-hover` `#152530`.

The hero carries a text ramp of its own because it is a brighter luminance
environment — that is what the `--hero-*` block is for. `--text-muted` composited
over the translucent hero card against the brightest gradient stop lands at 3.61
and fails; `--hero-muted` `#B6D5DB` is the same role at the luminance it needs,
shared with the tag chips.

Every colour was verified against the surface behind it, including each gradient
stop and each translucent surface composited over it. Worst case is 5.16.

## Constraints
- Dark only — one theme, no toggle, no `prefers-color-scheme`
- Static export (GitHub Pages), Next.js + Tailwind CSS
- Serif headings (Young Serif) + Sans body (Albert Sans) with Noto Sans/Serif TC for CJK
- Young Serif ships weight 400 only and `font-synthesis-weight` is `none`, so no
  serif element declares a weight — hierarchy is size and space. Enforced by
  `src/lib/typography.test.ts`
- Widths are a scale of four: 1100px (nav, hero), 880px (list sections =
  128 rail + 32 gutter + 720 content), 640px (the contact coda, the search
  field), 68ch (long-form prose *and* the hero's bio — both are paragraphs you
  read rather than scan, so they share a measure)
- Vertical rhythm is three steps, by weight rather than one value everywhere:
  112px (hero), 96px (content sections), 72px (codas, 404)
- Type is six steps: 11px mono, 13px metadata, 16px body, 20px card title,
  28px heading, plus the hero's clamp. Two exceptions are named in
  `design-system.test.ts` rather than waved through — an exception that has to
  say its own name cannot quietly become a back door
- The instrument rail holds what locates the unit beside it: a number in a
  sequence, a date in time, a category or count. `RailGrid` owns the geometry
  and nothing else may restate it. Home's numbers are counted off the sections
  actually rendered, so a conditional section cannot leave a hole
- Motion: two durations and two curves. `--dur-color`/`ease` for colour,
  `--dur-move`/`--ease-move` for anything that moves. Buttons lift
  (`translateY`), rows shift (`padding-left`), and neither crosses over
